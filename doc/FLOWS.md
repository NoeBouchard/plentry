# Flows

Step-by-step so agents do not invent a second checkout or a second Ops path. Screens live in `plentry/index.html`.

## Account

**Log in** in the header opens email and password. **Don't have an account?** under that form opens sign-up (name, email, password). Sign-up still offers **Already have an account? Log in**. Onboarding does not ask for an account. Without an account, New week, choosing or swapping a dinner, meal details, and the meal advisor open sign-up, as does confirm when the basket is ready (**Create an account…**). The later guard whose copy is **Log in so we can take the card hold** opens login.

## 1. First customer — hold

1. Onboarding: shop → what you eat (everything / vegetarian / vegan / pescatarian) → dinner tags (meal prep is not offered unless already saved) → household (1–6, and a saved `4+`; cooking for becomes 2, 4, or 6 unless that person already chose a number; UK postcode) → dinners 1–7 → cupboard ticks. Each cupboard row has a same-origin picture (a fallback jar if the name has none). Goals and the budget slider are not steps. A new account has no budget, so New week has no ×1.2 cap and the week screen has no under/over line. A saved number still does both. Profile can set one, and an empty budget field clears it. Saved goals still apply for people who already set them.
2. Menu shows up to N verified dinners, each **serves** the week’s locked servings. Changing cooking for asks before applying the new number to this week. The prompt says the basket will be rescaled and any changes made to it will be reset. Declining leaves this week locked, and the next week you build uses the new number. Boot or login shows that same prompt once when an implicit 2 becomes 4 or 6 and this week is still locked at the old number; either answer is remembered on `prefs.servingsWeekAsked`. Cooking instructions list scaled ingredient amounts for that number above the steps. Step text stays written for 2, with the factor (×2 for 4, ×3 for 6). A stored AI method written for a different number says that number and the multiply factor. A meal with no portions lists ingredient names. The week is shorter when fewer published meals fit the diet. On 21 Oct 2026 vegan has 6 reviewed dinners, so a 7-dinner vegan week is 6 long. Vegetarian can use vegetarian and vegan (23). Pescatarian drops meat (31). Omnivore can use all 52 reviewed dinners. Each day card shows that dinner’s penny share of this week’s grocery basket. The cards sum to **Basket £**. A pack only one dinner uses is charged to that dinner, including what is left in the pack. A shared pack — oil, a seasoning jar, or any pack more than one dinner draws from — is split by ingredient-cost weight: each dinner’s scaled portions ÷ pack × the shop’s first priced row (lowest id with a price). Seasonings are not inside that weight; the jar is still bought once and split by the weights. A dinner with no usable ingredient cost counts as one average-cost dinner when it shares a pack. Leftover pennies use largest remainder, and a tie goes to the earlier day. The weight does not depend on the rest of the week, so a dinner’s claim on a shared jar only moves when the other dinners that share it change. Under the cards: **Basket £** + delivery + 5% = the whole-pack total `pay` uses. A dinner with a missing shop price on a loaded shelf says **Price soon** to a customer and **incomplete** to an admin, and shows no price. If shelf prices have not loaded, or no shop is picked, the share uses the catalogue price the way the basket does. Then one line that the card hold adds a 30% buffer and only the actual amount is captured (the rest is released). **This shop** shows that total to the penny. The headline price is based on the meal basket, not a basket the user has edited. Time is the 2-serving minutes, plus 5 for 4 and plus 10 for 6. **Modify** lists 4–6 other **verified** catalog meals (6 when at least 6 fit the diet and are not already in the week), ranked with the same diet and tag weights as New week. Equal scores break with this week's seed: one mulberry32 key per meal id, then the id. Each New week tap mints a new seed, so consecutive taps usually differ. A shared `?seed=` link, or `setWeekSeed`, pins the sequence: the first New week uses that seed, and each further tap steps to the next number. Modify stays on the seed of the week on screen. **Show 4 more** lists up to 4 further meals and skips any alternative already shown for that slot. Fewer than 4 shows what is left. None left is a short message and a disabled refresh; **Start again** brings the first page back once the list has moved on. A week that already holds every matching dinner (6 vegan dinners, all in the week) shows that message and no empty grid. No AI wait. Picking one swaps that slot and rebuilds the basket and the basket share. A dinner that owns no basket line, because every ingredient it uses is ticked in the cupboard, shows **From your cupboard** on the day card and on a Modify option. A share that rounds to 0p while the dinner still owns a line shows the price, including £0.00. The week stays N dinners. A saved dinner that is hidden, unpublished, or no longer in the catalog is swapped for a visible meal when the week loads (same category when that dinner had one), the customer sees how many were swapped, and the cleaned week is saved. A successful catalog read does not add built-in dinners that are missing from the database. Checkout does not save the order if a dinner in the week is hidden or unknown; it runs that swap immediately, shows the same notice, and the customer can retry.
3. **Order this week** / Basket: skip cupboard spices already ticked; quantities from pack math for the shop in use; a line shows packs (`2 × 500g`). The shop comparison prices each supermarket with that shop's own packs. Changing shop rebuilds an unedited basket. Optional qty edits stay on the confirm screen. The card hold is still the derived total for the chosen shop.
4. Confirm: **door address + UK phone** (not card billing) and a **2-hour delivery window** (day ≥ today+3 Europe/London, 08:00–22:00). After they pick a window, warn: **be in** — the driver will call. Saved onto `prefs.delivery` and `orders.address.delivery`; slot onto `slot_date` / `slot_start` / `slot_end`. They choose the day first, then the time. A time tap does not select the earliest day. Pay stays disabled until both are chosen. Choosing either keeps a half-typed door address.
5. `pay` `checkout`: when the order names meals, **replaces quantities** with `basketFor` (database `meals.portions`, `items.servings`, `items.cupboard`, and that store's `pack_qty` / `pack_unit` when the unit matches) and then **rebuilds the basket server-side** (catalog keys, shelf product names/prices, whitelisted store search links). An order with no meal names keeps the stored basket. The confirm modal hold is that same derived total for the chosen shop. Writes `total` + `items` (including `servings`) back; expires any earlier Stripe session for this order; then Stripe Checkout (manual capture, cards only). Billing address is Stripe’s. Hold ≈ (basket + 5%) × 1.30. The client only follows a `https://checkout.stripe.com` URL.
6. Webhook `checkout.session.completed` → `payment_status=authorized` **only if** the row is still `unpaid`/`none`/`canceled` (keeps in-app delivery; records the real hold + session id; DB errors are 5xx so Stripe retries). Telegram fires **once**, list built from the row `pay` wrote, **PHONE:** first, **MUST book:** the promised window.
7. Customer lands on **Orders** (`?paid=1`). Timeline: hollow until done. Copy: **We'll deliver {window}**. **Issue** on any paid order. Each dinner has a **Cooking instructions** button (method snapshotted on the order).

The row itself is born `unpaid` / `new` with no hold — the DB trigger forces those on INSERT whatever the client sends.

## 2. Returning customer — same door?

Confirm shows **Deliver to this address?** (name, address, **phone**) when Profile has a complete door address including a UK phone. Missing phone (or any required door field) skips the reuse card and shows the form. **Use a different address** edits the form (pre-filled) and can **Use the saved address** to go back. Paying writes the chosen address to the profile again. The slot picker is always on Confirm. It is a required day-then-time step: time stays off until a day is chosen, and pay stays off until both are chosen. A day that has left the window is cleared, with its time. The disabled pay button says to choose a day and time.

## 3. Ops — shop and capture

1. Founder email only (`noyouchka.bouchard@gmail.com`). Once 2-step verification is on (Profile → **Security**), each login asks for the 6-digit code **before** Ops / Meals appear. The code sheet stays up until the code succeeds (tapping the dimmed area does not close it). Nav **Ops**.
2. Unpaid: faded, status locked, **do not shop**.
3. Authorized: **Phone** (the number the driver will call) then **Deliver to** the door address. Copy list / Open {store}. Shop with the **Wise (or other) float** — Stripe balance is not a Tesco card. Start a **new** supermarket checkout for this row; paste **this order’s** name, address, and **customer phone**. Do not rewrite the account-default home address.
4. Type till £ → preview customer charge (till + 5%) → **Charge £X** → **Confirm £X**. If Stripe captured but the order row did not save, Ops sees the error and the card stays on the charge step. A retry does not capture again once the PaymentIntent has succeeded.
5. Captured: **Money received — £X is in Stripe**. If the supermarket has the **promised window**, **Confirm this window** (marks **Ordered**; customer sees “{store} delivers {window}”). If it does not, **Message customer** (Inbox thread, `issue_status=open`) and wait — do not book a different slot. Later tap **Delivered**.
6. Never type a till whose +5% is above `amount_held` unless you mean to — in that case Ops charges the **hold** and Plentry covers the rest from the float.

£1.00 in Ops is a **money test** (charges £1.05). Do not place a supermarket order for that test.

## Customer receipt — did it all arrive? (written, not live)

`supabase/order_received.sql` was **applied to prod on 28 Oct 2026 at ~15:35:50 GMT**. Status values stay new, ordered, delivered. An order is complete when `received_confirmed_at` is set. After that file is applied and this static is promoted:

1. The Orders badge counts only the signed-in customer's own delivered orders that are not yet confirmed.
2. Those cards ask **Did it all arrive?** **Yes, complete** calls `confirm_order_received` (`received_confirmed_by = 'customer'`, and the row must be that customer's). The button disables while the request is in flight. A failure says **We couldn't confirm this just now, please try again**, enables the button, and reloads the list. **Something's wrong** opens the existing issue thread. The line says auto-completes in X hours, counted from `delivered_at` plus 48. An open issue replaces that line with **Paused while an issue is open**.
3. The timeline is Payment, Ordered, Delivered, **Complete**. Complete is green once `received_confirmed_at` is set. At 320px those four labels stay on one line.
4. Ops badges are **Awaiting receipt (Xh left)** and **Complete ✓ (customer, auto, ops, or backfill)**. An open issue shows **Paused while an issue is open** instead of the hours. Chips filter All, Awaiting receipt, and Complete. **Mark complete** calls `admin_mark_received` (`received_confirmed_by = 'ops'`) and the database checks `is_admin_aal2()`. A status change that the database rejects is shown to Ops.
5. `auto_complete_orders` runs every 15 minutes. It skips rows whose `issue_status` is `open` (the only open value; null and `resolved` do not pause). Resolving the issue does not move `delivered_at`, so a delivery that is already 48 hours old completes on the next sweep.
6. Applying the file marks a delivered, unconfirmed order `backfill` only when `delivered_at` is null and the issue is not open. That includes historic #31 and #38, and #39 when its issue is not open. A later apply does not confirm a live delivery. An open issue stays awaiting receipt. PR #34's completion-email backfill must treat `backfill` rows as already emailed or skipped.

## 4. Issue + Inbox

Customer **Issue** on a paid order opens the thread. Founder nav **Inbox** (open-count badge) lists open threads; **Message customer** on an Ops card starts one. Poll while the modal is open. Customer messages also Telegram-ping. Founder can **Resolve**.

## 5. Meals — publish a dinner

Logged-out and signed-in catalog reads use production policy `read published meals` (`reviewed_at is not null`) for anon and authenticated. Anon can select only `id, name, emoji, time, ing, image_url, recipe, tags, reviewed_at, portions, category`. Drafts stay off those reads. `supabase/meals_read_published.sql` was **applied** to prod at 12:59 GMT on 25 Oct 2026 (not a migration). The admin-with-MFA draft visibility check is untested. `supabase/meals_followups.sql` was **applied** to prod at about 15:42 GMT on 26 Oct 2026 (not a migration). Authenticated has the same 11 columns, loses DELETE, TRUNCATE, REFERENCES, and TRIGGER, and keeps INSERT and UPDATE. An aal2 admin reads `source`, `created_by`, and `created_at` (including drafts) through `admin_read_meals()`. That aal2 path is untested. Anon and authenticated still hold `MAINTAIN` on `public.meals`. Any new public catalogue column on `meals` must be added to the anon and authenticated column grants. Admin read of drafts stays `admin read meals` (`is_admin_aal2()`).

**Meals** tab (an admin: a row in `public.admins`, at aal2). **New meal** = `reviewed_at` null. Add is blocked until the dinner has a name, catalog ingredients, and at least one instruction step. **Verify & publish** also needs a category and sets `reviewed_at`. Customers can pick it on New week / Modify with no deploy. Unpublish returns it to New meal. There is no delete: a customer's current week names the dinner, and a deleted row is built by `pay` with an empty ingredient list, so the hold is short. The live list is grouped by the 7 categories. An expanded card shows the 2, 4, and 6 serving lists the customer sees. Seasoning keys already on the meal stay on save. New admins are not added in the app.

**Ingredients** tab (same admin gate). Prices and per-shop pack quantity/unit for Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, and Waitrose. Each shop that already has a row is updated on its own. A shop with no row is inserted as a full row, reusing that group's slug and category (including `grocery` and `fresh`). The client sends those as separate statements, not one transaction. Add is blocked until shop, category, price, and a meal key are set. Editing an existing row does not require a meal key. The shelf product name changes only when that shop's name was edited to a non-empty value; a blank field leaves the stored name. The pack label is written only when it changed. A new shop row needs a shelf product name. A key that is not in the code catalog is saved and marked not yet usable in baskets.

## 6. Advisor

Advisor chat is the only meal-generation AI. It may insert drafts (`reviewed_at` null). Those new dinners stay in the review queue and are not added to the week. The customer sees “We've sent this idea to our kitchen to review.” A proposal that is already a published catalog dinner can still be added. Adding those fills empty days then replaces from Monday, **still capped at N**. It must not call `meal_options` for Modify.

## 7. What not to “fix”

- Do not send the customer to pay Tesco themselves.
- Do not take the Stripe payout as the Tesco float (first payout is days later; Instant Payouts do not skip that).
- Do not use Stripe shipping as the door address when `address.delivery` exists.
- Do not grow the week past `prefs.meals`.
- Do not verify meals by editing SQL in chat when the Meals tab exists.
