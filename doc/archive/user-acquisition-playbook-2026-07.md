## Channel 1 — r/UKFrugal (post yourself, data first)

Rules verified 6 Jul: no coupons/referral codes, no cross-posting the same thread to multiple subs, title must summarise the topic, be nice. **No explicit self-promo ban — but the culture kills ad-shaped posts.** So the post is the data; the tool is a footnote.

Also fits the sub perfectly — today's top post is someone building a £9.87 food week.

>

## Channel 2 — Facebook budget-cooking groups (admin-first)

Target: **"Feed Your Family on a Budget"-type groups**, starting with Lorna Cooper's Feed Your Family for £20 a Week community (500k+ followers; her stated method is literally "shopping around, using more than one supermarket, planning your meals"). Group rules vary and most ban unapproved promo — message the admin first, always.

**Draft admin DM:**

> Hi — I'm Noe, solo founder of a free UK site (Plentry) that plans the week's dinners and shows the same basket priced at Sainsbury's, Asda and Waitrose, so you can see which store is cheapest before you shop. This week's 5-dinner plan came out £11.73 apart between stores.
>
> I'd love to offer it to your members — completely free, no codes, no catch (it's in beta and I want feedback more than anything). Would you be open to me posting a price comparison in the group, or would you rather try it yourself first? Happy to do whatever fits your rules.

If an admin engages, offer them early access before the group post — an admin's own post converts far better than yours.

## Before sending traffic

- Add `?ref=reddit` / `?ref=fb` etc. to shared links so you know which channel converts (log ref into the profile/state at signup).
- Confirm Telegram order notifications fire (a stranger's order you don't see = worst first impression).
- Post from your real, aged Reddit account, not a fresh one — fresh accounts get auto-filtered.
- Expect the first orders to expose friction (address capture, store choice, substitutions). Log every stumble — that's the roadmap.
